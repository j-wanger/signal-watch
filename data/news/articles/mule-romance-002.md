A long-running investment-romance fraud directed hundreds of victims to a bogus trading platform and then laundered their deposits through a network of money-mule accounts, according to a civil complaint filed this week.

The platform, marketed under the name Sterling Crest Capital, promised guaranteed returns and pressured victims to deposit ever-larger sums, the complaint says. Investigators allege a recruiter, Olena Hrytsenko, opened accounts in the names of others and used them to receive and immediately forward incoming funds.

One mule account, opened in the name of Nguyen Van Thanh, received deposits from dozens of unrelated senders over a short period and forwarded the funds within hours, the complaint states. Account records showed funds received from multiple unrelated originators and consolidated into a single account before being moved on, with no apparent economic rationale for the transfers.

The complaint describes deposits that were rapidly converted to virtual currency and moved off-platform, and accounts that were dormant before suddenly receiving high-volume inflows. Several accounts shared a common address and device, the filing notes.
